A genome study of Pokémon Emerald

What if a game cartridge were sequenced like an organism? These are the usual figures of a genome paper, measured on one game. The numbers are real, the biology is a joke: a chromosome here is a best guess at what a part of the ROM holds, and those labels can be wrong.

The figures read the decoded ROM at 4 bases per byte. The FASTA file itself stores compressed data, which looks like random sequence. The figures hold only measurements, never any part of the game.

Curiosity: Emerald's genome twin

Pokémon EmeraldOikopleura dioica
Genome67.1 Mbp65–70 Mbp
Chromosomes6 (best guesses)3 pairs
Life cycleUntil you stop playingAbout 4 days at 23 °C

Oikopleura dioica is a tiny tadpole-shaped sea creature that filters food through a mucus house it keeps abandoning and rebuilding. It is a tunicate, the sister group of vertebrates, and has one of the smallest genomes of any sequenced animal. The match is only in size: Emerald reaches 67.1 Mbp at 4 bases per byte, and estimates for the animal vary between populations and methods. Sources: Bliznina et al., 2021, larvaceans on Wikipedia.
